\section{Exact domains for the retained numerical profiles}
\label{sec:exact-domains}
This section lifts the actual retained operators into mathematical domains. It
defines their values, singularities and conditional invariants; it does not
install an exact runtime or claim a new physical accuracy result. The retained
orbital, navigation and coupled chapters remain dated implementation evidence.
Their reported measurements were not repeated for this subversion.

\subsection{Values, comparison and the meaning of a lift}
\label{sec:lift-values}
An imported finite binary64 value denotes its exact dyadic rational. Its original
bits are a separate serialization value: positive and negative zero are the same
real number, but have different bit patterns. Exact decimal constants, an exact
$\pi$, and the stored approximations to those constants are distinct atoms. A
specification must choose which atoms it uses. In the ideal geometric formulas
below, $\pi$ and the elementary functions have their mathematical meanings;
substituting them for rounded runtime constants is an explicit semantic change.

Integers, rationals and real algebraic numbers close the finite word, rational
matrix and square-root constructions. Trigonometric and logarithmic coordinates
require explicit real transcendental expressions. A finite recurrence is another
finite expression, even when expensive to expand. An implicit operator
$\operatorname{ROOT}$ or $\operatorname{FLOW}$ instead denotes a solution of stated
equations, subject to existence and selection conditions. Naming an implicit
operator supplies neither a closed-form solver nor a terminating evaluator.

Comparisons of exact rational or algebraic values are decidable. A general
transcendental expression has a mathematical order, but this specification does
not supply a universal decision procedure for it. An evaluator must retain an
unresolved comparison obligation when its method cannot establish the answer.
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 n=\lfloor x\rfloor\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\in\mathbb Z,
 \qquad n\le x<n+1 .
\end{equation}
No epsilon replaces equality at a cycle, quantization or event boundary.
Piecewise expressions are lazy: an inactive force term does not require its
unused denominator. These lifts remove floating-point rounding, overflow and
underflow; consequently, they do not preserve every numerical branch or establish
bit-equivalence to R10. Bit preservation belongs to the unchanged serialized
payload and word codec, not to recomputation of its real-valued consequences.

\subsection{Coefficient blocks, Earth frames and force evaluation}
\label{sec:lift-orbit}
The source map is \code{orbit\_dynamics.py}, \code{orbit\_precision.py} and
\code{orbit\_kernel.hpp} in the preserved R10 tree. The complete seed fields and
physical scope are retained in Section~\ref{sec:orbit}. Let a coefficient block
cover $[a,b]$, with $b>a$, and put $u=(2t-a-b)/(b-a)$. If several blocks contain
$t$, the block with greatest stored index wins, including a shared endpoint.
For each scalar row,
\begin{align}
 p(t)&=\sum_{k=0}^{d}c_kT_k(u), &
 T_0&=1,\quad T_1=u,\quad T_{k+1}=2uT_k-T_{k-1},\\
 b_{d+1}&=b_{d+2}=0, &
 b_k&=c_k+2u b_{k+1}-b_{k+2}\quad(k=d,\ldots,1),\\
 p(t)&=c_0+u b_1-b_2, &
 \dot p(t)&=\frac{2}{b-a}\sum_{k=1}^{d}k c_kU_{k-1}(u).
\end{align}
For degree zero the value is $c_0$ and the derivative is zero. The finite
Clenshaw recurrence equals the Chebyshev sum by backward substitution; formal
differentiation gives the derivative. No continuity between separately fitted
blocks follows from this identity. A timestamp outside all blocks is undefined.

Write $c_x=\cos x_p$, $s_x=\sin x_p$, and similarly for $y_p$. The actual passive
frame convention is
\begin{equation}
 P=\begin{pmatrix}c_x&0&s_x\\s_y s_x&c_y&-s_y c_x\\-c_y s_x&s_y&c_y c_x\end{pmatrix},
 \quad R(a)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos a&\sin a&0\\-\sin a&\cos a&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \quad M(t)=P(t)R(a(t))Q(t).
\end{equation}
R1 uses constant polar motion and $a=a_0+\omega t$. R2 uses stored EOP rows
$e_0,x_p,y_p$, with $a=a_0+\omega t+e_0(t)$. The nine entries of $Q$ are stored
polynomial rows, rather than a symbolic rotation parameterization. Position and
velocity transform as
\begin{equation}
 r_E=Mr,\qquad v_E=Mv+\dot Mr,\qquad
 \dot M=\dot PRQ+P\dot RQ+PR\dot Q.
\end{equation}
\textbf{Conditional invariant.} Exact trigonometry gives $P^TP=R^TR=I$ and
determinant $+1$. Thus $M^TM=Q^TQ$: length preservation and the identification of
$M^T$ with $M^{-1}$ require a separate proof that $Q^TQ=I$. Independent polynomial
fits do not supply that proof. Applying $M^T$ to a force remains the specified
algebraic operation even when this ideal rotation condition is absent; silently
orthogonalizing $Q$ would define a different model.

For the R2 external gravity field let $p=Mr$, $R=\|p\|$, and let $a_E>0$ denote
the reference radius. The declared profile requires $R>a_E$, in addition to
nonzero denominators. Its finite Cartesian solid-harmonic construction is
\begin{align}
 X&=a_Ep_x/R^2,&Y&=a_Ep_y/R^2,&Z&=a_Ep_z/R^2,&D&=a_E^2/R^2,\\
 V_{00}&=a_E/R,&W_{00}&=0,\\
 V_{mm}&=(2m-1)(XV_{m-1,m-1}-YW_{m-1,m-1}),\\
 W_{mm}&=(2m-1)(XW_{m-1,m-1}+YV_{m-1,m-1}),\\
 V_{nm}&=\frac{2n-1}{n-m}ZV_{n-1,m}
       -\frac{n+m-1}{n-m}DV_{n-2,m}\quad(n>m),\\
 U(p)&=\frac{\mu}{a_E}\sum_{n=0}^{d}\sum_{m=0}^{n}
       (C_{nm}V_{nm}+S_{nm}W_{nm}),\qquad a_g=M^T\nabla_pU.
\end{align}
The $W_{nm}$ off-diagonal recurrence is identical with $V$ replaced by $W$; omit
the second term when $n=m+1$. The retained R2 degree is at most 12, $C_{00}=1$,
$S_{00}=S_{n0}=0$, and the convention has no Condon--Shortley phase. Conversion
from the documented fully normalized source is
\begin{equation}
 C_{nm}=\sqrt{(2-\delta_{m0})(2n+1)\frac{(n-m)!}{(n+m)!}}\,\bar C_{nm},
 \qquad S_{nm}\text{ uses the same factor}.
\end{equation}
The native forward derivative differentiates this finite recurrence by the sum,
product and chain rules. In contrast, the Python construction/reference path
uses $\operatorname{Im}U(p+i\epsilon e_j)/\epsilon$ with fixed
$\epsilon=10^{-6}$ in its coordinate units. Exact arithmetic at a nonzero
$\epsilon$ does not turn that expression into $\partial_jU$. Its analytic
limit, where the complex extension is regular, defines the derivative; the
finite complex-step operator and the exact derivative must retain different names.

The older R1 gravity formula is also explicit. For $s=p_z/R$ and unnormalized
Legendre polynomials
$P_2=(3s^2-1)/2$, $P_3=(5s^3-3s)/2$ and
$P_4=(35s^4-30s^2+3)/8$, put
\begin{align}
 a_{E,1}&=-\mu p/R^3+
 \sum_{n=2}^{4}\frac{\mu J_na_E^n}{R^{n+3}}
 \bigl(((n+1)P_n+sP_n')p-RP_n'e_z\bigr)\\
 &\quad+\frac{\mu a_E^2}{R^5}
       \left(\nabla T-\frac{5T}{R^2}p\right),\qquad
 T=3C_{22}(p_x^2-p_y^2)+6S_{22}p_xp_y.
\end{align}
Its inertial gravity is $M^Ta_{E,1}$. This is a separate finite field, not a
claim that the R1 and R2 coefficient conventions are interchangeable.

Both orbital profiles add the declared active terms below; the relativistic
term is an R2 option. Sun and Moon vectors $r_b(t)$ come from their seed
coefficient blocks. With $r_*=\|r\|$, $d=r-r_\odot$, and $\hat s=r_\odot/\|r_\odot\|$,
\begin{align}
 a_b&=\mu_b\left(\frac{r_b-r}{\|r_b-r\|^3}-\frac{r_b}{\|r_b\|^3}\right),\\
 a_{\rm SRP}&=\chi A\frac{\mathrm{AU}^2}{\|d\|^3}d,
 &\chi&=\begin{cases}0,&\text{shadow enabled},\ r\cdot\hat s<0,
       \ \|r-(r\cdot\hat s)\hat s\|<a_E,\\1,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\\
 \hat R&=r/r_*,\quad \hat N=(r\times v)/\|r\times v\|,
 &a_{\rm RTN}&=a_R\hat R+a_T(\hat N\times\hat R)+a_N\hat N,\\
 a_{\rm GR}&=\frac{\mu}{c^2r_*^3}
 \left[\left(\frac{4\mu}{r_*}-v\cdot v\right)r+4(r\cdot v)v\right].
\end{align}
Active body terms require $r_b\ne0$ and $r\ne r_b$; SRP requires its Sun and
displacement denominators, RTN requires $r_*\ne0$ and $r\times v\ne0$, and GR
requires $r_*\ne0$ and $c>0$. An identically inactive RTN term does not impose
its normal-vector requirement. Equality on either shadow boundary is lit under
the retained strict inequalities. These formulas contain no unlisted maneuver,
penumbra, drag or higher-degree force. Exact evaluation cannot supply one.

\subsection{Exact discrete integration and a distinct continuous flow}
\label{sec:lift-flow}
Let $y=(r,v)$ and $F(t,y)=(v,a(t,r,v))$. The exact lift of the implemented step
is the finite operator $\Psi_h$:
\begin{align}
 k_1&=F(t,y),& k_2&=F(t+h/2,y+hk_1/2),\\
 k_3&=F(t+h/2,y+hk_2/2),& k_4&=F(t+h,y+hk_3),\\
 \Psi_h(t,y)&=y+\tfrac h6(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4).
\end{align}
All four stage arguments must belong to the active domain, including the
declared time interval and external-radius guard. For a positive lattice spacing
$h_0$ and elapsed time $\tau$, set $n=\operatorname{trunc}(\tau/h_0)$ toward zero,
and $\epsilon=\tau-nh_0$. Compose signed steps from epoch to $nh_0$, then apply
one step of size $\epsilon$ if nonzero. Fractional query results are never used
as canonical lattice states.

\textbf{Preservation proposition.} A cache returning only the states defined by
this same canonical recurrence, under the same seed and branch choices, leaves
every query unchanged. Proof is induction on the signed step index; the last
fractional step is a pure function of the canonical state. This does not imply
that arbitrary reblocking of RK4 steps, reversing a step, or changing the order
of force additions preserves the floating-point result. Even over exact reals,
RK4 generally has neither the continuous-flow semigroup law nor exact energy
conservation or time reversibility.

Define the separate implicit operator by
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{FLOW}_F(t,t_0,y_0)=y(t),\qquad
 y(t)=y_0+\int_{t_0}^{t}F(s,y(s))\,ds .
\end{equation}
On an open collision-free region with all active denominators bounded away
from zero, fixed force branches and coefficient blocks, $F$ is locally Lipschitz
in $y$ and continuous in $t$. The usual initial-value theorem then gives a
unique maximal local solution. Piecewise continuous time coefficients can be
concatenated across declared time boundaries under corresponding local
existence conditions. No continuity of separately fitted coefficients is
assumed. State-triggered shadow switches require a specified event solution
concept and additional uniqueness conditions; isolated transversal crossings
with a prescribed continuation and no accumulation of events are sufficient
local hypotheses. The literal boundary convention alone does not prove these
hypotheses for grazing, sliding or infinitely accumulating events.

When the unique flow exists on all intervening times, its composition law
$\operatorname{FLOW}(t_2,t_0,y)=\operatorname{FLOW}(t_2,t_1,
\operatorname{FLOW}(t_1,t_0,y))$ follows from uniqueness. It does not transfer to
$\Psi_h$. A seed specifying $F$ and $y_0$ defines an initial-value problem; it
is not a closed-form solution. Neither operator certifies agreement with a
physical satellite or extends the seed's declared domain.

\subsection{Ground stations, angles, visibility and light time}
\label{sec:lift-station}
The preserved \code{geodesy.py} and \code{orbit\_query.py} specify a fixed
ellipsoidal station. With semimajor axis $a>0$, flattening $0<f<1$,
$e^2=f(2-f)$, latitude $\varphi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$, longitude $\lambda$, and height $h$,
\begin{align}
 N&=a/\sqrt{1-e^2\sin^2\varphi},\\
 r_g&=((N+h)\cos\varphi\cos\lambda,
 (N+h)\cos\varphi\sin\lambda,(N(1-e^2)+h)\sin\varphi),\\
 L&=\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\lambda&\cos\lambda&0\\
 -\sin\varphi\cos\lambda&-\sin\varphi\sin\lambda&\cos\varphi\\
 \cos\varphi\cos\lambda&\cos\varphi\sin\lambda&\sin\varphi\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad z=L(r_E-r_g),\quad \dot z=Lv_E .
\end{align}
Exact trigonometry proves $LL^T=I$ and determinant $+1$; hence the station
translation and ENU rotation preserve pairwise Euclidean distances and have
inverse $r_E=r_g+L^Tz$. The retained inverse geodesy implementation is a separate
finite operator: outside its pole branch it starts with
$\varphi_0=\operatorname{atan2}(z,p(1-e^2))$, $p=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, and repeats
$\varphi_{j+1}=\operatorname{atan2}(z+e^2N(\varphi_j)\sin\varphi_j,p)$ at most
20 times, stopping at change below $10^{-14}$. Its pole threshold is
$p<10^{-10}$, with longitude zero; its height formula switches between
$p/\cos\varphi-N$ and $z/\sin\varphi-N(1-e^2)$ at $|\cos\varphi|>0.01$.
An implicit inverse of the forward ellipsoid chart needs its own uniqueness
domain and longitude convention; it is not identical by definition to these
20 iterations.

For $z=(E,N,U)$, set $H=\sqrt{E^2+N^2}$ and $D=\|z\|>0$. The simultaneous
geometric observables are
\begin{align}
 \alpha&=\operatorname{atan2}(E,N)\bmod2\pi,&
 \varepsilon&=\operatorname{atan2}(U,H),&
 \dot D&=z\cdot\dot z/D,\\
 \dot\alpha&=(N\dot E-E\dot N)/H^2,&
 \dot s&=\dot U/D-U\dot D/D^2,&s&=U/D .
\end{align}
Azimuth and its rate are intrinsically undefined at $H=0$. The retained query
policy publishes them only for $H>10^{-6}$ metres; otherwise they are absent.
Under that same policy, elevation rate is
$\dot s/\max(10^{-15},\cos\varepsilon)$, otherwise absent. The max and threshold
are named implementation choices, distinct from the ideal derivative
$\dot s/\cos\varepsilon$ on its nonsingular domain. Visibility uses
$\varepsilon\ge\varepsilon_{\min}$, so equality is visible. On a physical mask
in $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$, the event equation is $s-\sin\varepsilon_{\min}=0$.
An isolated bracket plus uniqueness or monotonicity conditions can specify one
root; a sampled sign-change search is not a proof that every event or tangent
contact was found.

This orbital station profile is geometric at one timestamp. It has no
light-time or atmospheric-refraction correction. The separate live GPS profile
below uses emission time and Earth rotation during flight. Their equations
cannot be merged merely by calling both results a ground-station position.

\subsection{UGTS charts, OTAN2, addresses and calendar winding}
\label{sec:lift-ugts}
The source \code{ugts.py} has distinct continuous charts and finite addresses.
For horizontal distance $H>0$ and scale $r_0>0$, the station chart is
$\rho=\log(H/r_0)$ and $\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(N,E)$; note that this angle
is measured from east, while station azimuth above is measured from north.
Chart inversion gives $E=r_0e^\rho\cos\theta$, $N=r_0e^\rho\sin\theta$.
The polar origin requires an explicit absent-chart status.
Define the half-open wrap, including its exact endpoint rule, by
\begin{equation}
 W_P(x)=x-P\left\lfloor\frac{x+P/2}{P}\right\rfloor\in[-P/2,P/2),
 \qquad P>0 .
\end{equation}
Thus $+P/2$ maps to $-P/2$ and $W_P(x+kP)=W_P(x)$ for every integer $k$.
The source OTAN2 is the literal ratio-angle operator
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal O(\Delta\rho,\Delta\vartheta;a_0)
   =\arctan(\Delta\vartheta/\Delta\rho)-a_0,
 \qquad \Delta\rho\ne0 .
\end{equation}
Both increments zero give \code{zero\_increment}; a zero radial increment with
nonzero angle increment gives \code{ratio\_undefined}. Missing chart history
is another status. It is not a quadrant-aware bearing and must not be silently
replaced by $\operatorname{atan2}$. The runtime's quotient-overflow/underflow
\code{numerical\_range} status has no analogue for an exact nonzero real
denominator; lifting this quotient consequently changes that numerical status.

For $\rho\in[-20,0]$, widths $(20,18,14,12)$ and integer tick $k$, the retained
quantizer is
\begin{align}
 q_\rho&=\left\lfloor\frac{\rho+20}{20}(2^{20}-1)+\frac12\right\rfloor,
 &Q_w(a)&=\left\lfloor\frac{(a\bmod2\pi)2^w}{2\pi}+\frac12\right\rfloor\bmod2^w,\\
 q_\theta&=Q_{18}(\theta),&q_t&=k\bmod2^{14},\qquad q_\phi=Q_{12}(\phi).
\end{align}
Ties round upward before angular reduction. The two codecs are exact
permutations of these 64 input bits. The contiguous codec is
\begin{equation}
 K_C=2^{44}q_\rho+2^{26}q_\theta+2^{12}q_t+q_\phi.
\end{equation}
The Morton codec enumerates depths $d=0,\ldots,19$; at each depth it enumerates
fields $i=0,\ldots,3$ with $d<w_i$, copying input bit $w_i-1-d$ into the next
output position, starting at bit 63. Every input bit occurs once, proving that
inverse extraction recovers the tuple exactly. A prefix fixes a leading bit
string in each field; appending all-zero or all-one suffixes gives the exact
Cartesian bounds on its possible tuples. This is a finite address statement,
not an isometric embedding of continuous space. Quantization is many-to-one;
nearest reconstruction has radial error at most $10/(2^{20}-1)$ and circular
angular error at most $\pi/2^w$, under exact comparisons and these domains.

Full time is retained separately from the periodic address. For integer ticks,
reference $k_0$ and positive integer period $P$, Euclidean division gives
\begin{equation}
 k-k_0=Pw+r,\qquad 0\le r<P,\qquad \text{phase}=r/P,
 \qquad k=k_0+Pw+r .
\end{equation}
The identity holds for negative ticks and exact boundaries: the next cycle has
remainder zero and winding increased by one. A phase without its winding does
not recover full time. Station queries use $k=\lfloor t_{\rm GPST}/\delta\rfloor$,
$\delta>0$, and the time address has period $2^{14}$. Gregorian leap rules use
integer divisibility by 4, excluding centuries except those divisible by 400;
the retained Gregorian formulas and 400-year evidence concern uniform civil-day
formatting. They do not supply a UTC leap-second table, turn February into a
fixed-length month, or extend orbital validity. The runtime formats finite
timestamps at millisecond precision; exact fractional seconds and exact calendar
integers require a separately specified formatter.

The two topological chart profiles must remain distinct. With $L=\rho_h-\rho_l>0$,
$j=\lfloor(\rho-\rho_l)/L\rfloor$ and $\rho'=\rho-jL$, odd $j$ applies either
$(\theta,\phi)\mapsto(\theta+\pi,-\phi)$ in \code{source\_half\_turn}, or
$(\theta,\phi)\mapsto(\pi-\theta,-\phi)$ in \code{reflective\_klein}; both flip
the stored orientation sign and wrap the angles. Even $j$ leaves them unchanged.
The floor proves $\rho'\in[\rho_l,\rho_h)$; the angular transformations are
involutions modulo full turns. Their different coordinate actions are not
interchangeable physical reflections.

For completeness, geometric predicate construction is also explicit. A segment
distance uses $d=b-a$ and
$u=\operatorname{clamp}((p-a)\cdot d/(d\cdot d),0,1)$, or $u=0$ when $d=0$,
then $\|p-a-ud\|$. A sphere uses $\|p-c\|-R$. The finite cone has unit axis $n$,
slant length $\ell>0$ and $0<\alpha<\pi/2$. Put
$z=(p-a)\cdot n$, $q=\|p-a-zn\|$, $h=\ell\cos\alpha$, $R=\ell\sin\alpha$.
Its signed distance is the minimum distance from $(q,z)$ to the three edges of
the meridian triangle $(-R,h),(R,h),(0,0)$, with negative sign exactly when
$0\le z\le h$ and $q\le z\tan\alpha$. These are algebraic/transcendental
piecewise constructions, with explicit unit-axis and cone-angle conditions.
For a 1-Lipschitz signed distance $f$ swept along a segment from $a$ to $b$,
$N\ge2$ uniform samples yield
\begin{equation}
 m-\frac{\|b-a\|}{2(N-1)}\ \le\
 \min_{s\in[a,b]}f(p-s)\ \le m,
 \qquad m=\min_{0\le j<N}f\left(p-a-\frac{j(b-a)}{N-1}\right).
\end{equation}
The proof chooses the nearest sample, at distance at most half a sample gap,
and applies Lipschitz continuity. Ordinary non-directed floating-point
evaluation of the displayed endpoints is not thereby a certified enclosure.
A Boolean perturbation $f+\eta(2b-1)$, $b\in\{0,1\}$ and $\eta\ge0$, lies in
$[f-\eta,f+\eta]$; an interval centered on the perturbed measurement is a
different construction and must not replace this family envelope.

\subsection{SATNAV selection, QR and finite nonlinear iteration}
\label{sec:lift-satnav}
The numerical source is \code{include/satnav\_core.hpp}; the independent
\code{python/reference.py} uses Householder QR. Word operations below are on
fixed-width unsigned words. With present, ready, ASA, NA and boundary words,
\begin{equation}
 A=\text{present}\mathbin{\&}\text{ready}\mathbin{\&}\text{ASA},\quad
 B=A\mathbin{\&}\text{NA},\quad h=\operatorname{popcount}(B\mathbin{\&}\text{boundary}),
 \quad S=\begin{cases}0,&h>0,\\B,&h=0.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Any boundary hit absorbs the whole selected word. A hit gives
\code{MASK\_ABSORBED}; fewer than four selected channels gives
\code{TOO\_FEW}. Otherwise rows are consumed in increasing channel order.
For receiver state $x\in\mathbb R^3$, clock range $b$, satellite $s_i$,
corrected code $P_i$, and positive uncertainty weight $\sigma_i$,
\begin{equation}
 d_i=\|x-s_i\|,\quad r_i=P_i-d_i-b,\quad
 A_i=\frac1{\sigma_i}\left((x-s_i)^T/d_i,1\right),\quad
 z_i=r_i/\sigma_i .
\end{equation}
The native profile requires $d_i>1$ metre. The Python reference requires
$d_i>0$. Their mathematical least-squares equations coincide on the common
domain, while their accepted-input policies differ. This is retained explicitly.

The native online Givens update starts with zero triangular $R$ and transformed
right-hand side $z$. For an incoming row $a$ and right-hand side $b_r$, at
column $j$ let $\gamma=\sqrt{R_{jj}^2+a_j^2}$. If $\gamma=0$, skip that rotation;
otherwise put $c=R_{jj}/\gamma$, $s=a_j/\gamma$, and apply
\begin{equation}
 \binom{R'_{jk}}{a'_k}=\begin{pmatrix}c&s\\-s&c\end{pmatrix}
 \binom{R_{jk}}{a_k}\quad(k>j),\qquad
 \binom{z'_j}{b'_r}=\begin{pmatrix}c&s\\-s&c\end{pmatrix}
 \binom{z_j}{b_r},\qquad R'_{jj}=\gamma .
\end{equation}
Since $c^2+s^2=1$, each rotation preserves Euclidean residual norm. Sequential
elimination therefore gives the same linear least-squares minimizer as the
original whitened system whenever its four columns have full rank. Back
substitution is $\Delta_j=(z_j-\sum_{k>j}R_{jk}\Delta_k)/R_{jj}$.
The reference Householder construction instead uses
$H=I-2vv^T/(v^Tv)$ with $v=a-\alpha e_1$,
$\alpha=-\operatorname{copysign}(\|a\|,a_1)$ and a declared zero sign convention.
$H^TH=I$ proves the same minimizer on the common full-rank domain; QR factors
and floating-point results need not be identical.

The retained rejection policy is stronger than algebraic rank: it rejects if
any $|R_{jj}|\le\tau\max_k|R_{kk}|$, with configured positive relative threshold
$\tau$, or all diagonals vanish. A nonsingular system may therefore be rejected
by policy. At each accepted nonlinear pass $(x,b)\leftarrow(x,b)+\Delta$.
The configured finite loop, normally at most 12 passes, reports convergence
when $\|\Delta_{1:3}\|\le\epsilon_p$ and $|\Delta_4|\le\epsilon_b$.
An exact lift preserves this finite stopping rule as a predicate, not as proof
of an exact nonlinear root, global least-squares minimum or accurate position.

For full-rank final Jacobian, the nominal covariance is
$(A^TA)^{-1}=R^{-1}R^{-T}$; its diagonal entries are the squared row norms of
$R^{-1}$. This identity is algebraic, while interpreting it as an uncertainty
statement requires a correct statistical observation model. Final RMS,
$\sqrt{\sum r_i^2/m}$, weighted sum $\sum(r_i/\sigma_i)^2$, and maximum normalized
residual $\max|r_i/\sigma_i|$ are separate diagnostics. Four observations have no
redundancy; a residual threshold for more rows does not certify external
position error. Finite-limit, rank, missing-input and invalid-geometry statuses
remain part of the operator's tagged result.
