\section{A minimal physical-law layer for application kernels}
\label{sec:r13-core}
R13 strengthens aTOMos where a physical law supplies a missing relationship,
constrains a proposed coupling or justifies a useful implementation choice.
It is a focused supplement to R12. The existing XOP representation, literal
ASA/NA and JK rules, WANTWOMBAN packet, orbital and receiver equations remain
in their bound parent. Repeating that corpus would not strengthen these laws.

The new profile is \code{XOP-PHYSICS-R1}. Its mathematical bodies use the
existing \code{XOP-R1} operators; it does not allocate new opcodes or claim a
new executable backend. A physical specialization imports only the laws and
closures needed by its declared interface. A purely digital permutation does
not need a continuum electromagnetic model merely because real hardware will
eventually execute it.

\subsection{Five distinct parts of a physical specialization}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}P{30mm}Y Y@{}}
\toprule Part & What it supplies & Example in the existing model\\\midrule
Balance law & Conserved quantity, flux or production relation & Charge continuity; energy and momentum balance\\
Constitutive model & Material or component response under stated conditions & Polarization dynamics; thermoelastic free energy\\
Reduction & A finite or simplified model with its scope & Fixed modal basis; scalar optical field; lumped temperature\\
Discrete evolution & A selected finite state transition & Existing backward Euler; new energy-balanced midpoint\\
Observation & How physical state produces recorded evidence & Acquisition window, calibration, quantization and uncertainty\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}\end{center}
Conservation equations alone do not specify a material. A constitutive law alone
does not provide boundary data. Exact arithmetic alone does not select a
conservative time update. A recorded count alone does not fix a physical
measurand. Keeping these parts explicit allows specialization without hidden
changes of meaning.

\subsection{Normative profile contract}
A selected physical profile declares the finite record
\begin{equation}
 \Pi=(\mathcal T,\mathcal X,\mathcal U,\mathcal Y,\Theta,
       \mathcal B,\mathcal C,\mathcal A,\mathcal N,\mathcal O,\mathcal E).
\end{equation}
$\mathcal T$ contains units, frames and time conventions. $\mathcal X$ declares
state and initial conditions; $\mathcal U,\mathcal Y$ declare inputs and outputs.
$\Theta$ contains parameters with source identities and joint uncertainty.
$\mathcal B$ lists applicable balance laws. $\mathcal C$ supplies constitutive
closures. $\mathcal A$ lists approximation and admissibility hypotheses.
$\mathcal N$ identifies a finite representation and the chosen update.
$\mathcal O$ defines acquisition and calibrated observations.
$\mathcal E$ records the claimed output bounds and evidence status.

Each law entry has a qualified name/version, argument types, equation body,
domain, boundary terms, source and a status. Status distinguishes a definition,
a derived identity, an adopted physical law, a constitutive assumption and a
measured parameter. The same source tag cannot turn one status into another.
The editable normative field contract is \code{formal/PHYSICS\_PROFILE.md}.
This is a record definition, not a claim that a new parser implements it.

For a finite state model, the mathematical content can be written
\begin{equation}
 \dot x=f_\Pi(t,x,u;\Theta),\quad y=g_\Pi(t,x,u;\Theta),\quad
 B_j(t,x,\dot x,u;\Theta)=0,
 \label{eq:r13-profile}
\end{equation}
with declared inequalities and initial/boundary conditions. The $B_j$ are
relations to derive or impose consistently, not extra independent equations
to append blindly to a fully determined system. Redundant balances are
identified; inconsistent initial data or incompatible closures do not define
an admitted solution. A finite ODE must meet the existing existence and
uniqueness domain. Algebraic constraints need elimination or a separately
defined constrained formulation; they are not silently handled by \code{ODE}.

For example, a selector $b\in\B$ may choose
$K(b)=K_0+b(K_1-K_0)$. It does not by itself specify the work required to
switch stiffness. For an unchanged coordinate $q$, that storage change is
$q^T(K_1-K_0)q/2$ and must be attributed to the switching interaction.
Signed damping or stiffness remains mathematically expressible; a passive
energy theorem applies only when its sign hypotheses hold. An active element
needs its actual supply and constitutive model.

\subsection{Representation preserves laws only through an explicit map}
Let $x=\mathcal R(z)$ reconstruct a finite physical model from encoded state
$z$, and let $F_h$ be a chosen digital transition. A continuous-law identity
does not automatically give
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B_{j,h}\bigl(\mathcal R(z),\mathcal R(F_h(z,u)),u\bigr)=0.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal B_{j,h}$ is a separately defined discrete balance, rather than
the continuous residual $B_j$ with its arguments silently changed. The
discrete relation has to be derived for the selected step.
The law may instead provide an error bound or a nonzero residual ledger.
Likewise, a finite basis is a declared approximation to a continuum field:
exact evaluation of its coefficients and trajectory does not erase omitted
modes. The electromagnetic and mechanical chapters state the necessary
finite-representation and integral domains.

One-bit word packing preserves the encoded operators, parameters and inputs.
It does not turn a finite word mask into an electromagnetic absorber, identify
a code bit with a photon, or establish that a material follows a named law.
Those are explicit realization mappings. The R12 photonic refinement contract
continues to govern any proposed hardware executor.

\section{An exact finite step with a derived mechanical energy balance}
\label{sec:r13-step}
This addition supplies a specific useful kernel specialization. It removes
algorithmic energy loss from the fixed linear model's discrete balance while
retaining physical damping. It does not replace the existing CGK or orbital
profiles silently and does not assert exact continuous-time trajectories.

\subsection{Selected model and units}
Choose a finite number of length-valued coordinates $q$ in a fixed declared
modal basis, velocity $v$, and a fixed reference configuration. Let
\begin{equation}
 \dot q=v,\qquad M\dot v=\bar f-Kq-Cv,
 \quad M=M^T\succ0,\quad K=K^T\succeq0,\quad C=C^T\succeq0.
 \label{eq:r13-linear-model}
\end{equation}
Here the displayed $\bar f$ denotes the force selected for the interval's
discrete supply; a held-force physical model may use the same constant value.
The quantities $M,C,K$ have units $\mathrm{kg}$, $\mathrm{kg/s}$ and
$\mathrm{kg/s^2}$ when all $q$ coordinates have length units. Generalized
coordinates of mixed dimensions require the inherited tuple/block rules,
not a false uniformly dimensioned matrix. The storage is
\begin{equation}
 E(q,v)=\tfrac12v^TMv+\tfrac12q^TKq,
 \qquad \dot E=v^T\bar f-v^TCv.
 \label{eq:r13-mech-storage}
\end{equation}
The continuous identity follows by differentiating the two quadratic terms
and substituting the model. Its damping term becomes heat or another named
output store in a coupled physical model, as derived in the energy chapter.

The parent already defines backward Euler for a more general signed CGK
profile. Under fixed passive coefficients its storage change includes
\begin{equation}
 -h(v^+)^TCv^+
 -\tfrac12(v^+-v)^TM(v^+-v)
 -\tfrac12(q^+-q)^TK(q^+-q)
\end{equation}
in the unforced case. The last two terms are numerical dissipation from the
selected update. Exact arithmetic preserves those terms exactly; it does not
remove them. They cannot be reassigned to measured physical heat without a
separate modeling decision. R13 therefore names an alternative finite profile.

\subsection{PHYS-MIDPOINT-LINEAR-R1}
For $h\ge0$, write $\bar q=(q^++q)/2$ and $\bar v=(v^++v)/2$. Define
\begin{equation}
 q^+-q=h\bar v,\qquad
 M(v^+-v)=h(\bar f-K\bar q-C\bar v).
 \label{eq:r13-midpoint}
\end{equation}
These undivided equations also define the $h=0$ identity step. Eliminate
$q^+$ to obtain the explicit finite solve
\begin{align}
 A_h&=M+\tfrac h2C+\tfrac{h^2}{4}K,\\
 b_h&=(M-\tfrac h2C-\tfrac{h^2}{4}K)v-hKq+h\bar f,\\
 v^+&=A_h^{-1}b_h,\qquad q^+=q+\tfrac h2(v^++v).
 \label{eq:r13-midpoint-solve}
\end{align}
\begin{proposition}[Existence and exact field closure]
For the stated symmetric sign conditions and $h\ge0$, $A_h$ is positive
definite. Consequently the step is uniquely defined. If all supplied entries
are rational, both output vectors have exact rational entries.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $w\ne0$, $w^TA_hw=w^TMw+(h/2)w^TCw+(h^2/4)w^TKw>0$.
Thus $A_h$ is invertible. A finite nonsingular linear solve over the rationals
uses rational field operations and has rational output. No real root or
transcendental decision is required for this selected rational profile.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Discrete power balance]
For the same fixed model,
\begin{equation}
 E(q^+,v^+)-E(q,v)
       =h\bar v^T\bar f-h\bar v^TC\bar v.
 \label{eq:r13-midpoint-energy}
\end{equation}
In particular, unforced undamped storage is preserved exactly, while the
unforced damped step cannot increase it.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Symmetry gives
$\Delta E=\bar v^TM(v^+-v)+\bar q^TK(q^+-q)$.
Substitute \eqref{eq:r13-midpoint}; the stiffness terms cancel, leaving
\eqref{eq:r13-midpoint-energy}. Nonnegativity follows from $C\succeq0$.
\end{proof}
The supplied work $h\bar v^T\bar f$ and dissipated energy $h\bar v^TC\bar v$
are the discrete profile's quantities. They are not automatically the exact
work and heat integrals of the continuous trajectory. The theorem is a
storage identity, not a proof of arbitrary-step trajectory accuracy or a
guarantee about every other invariant.

\subsection{Exact seed body and causal integration}
The template arguments are
\begin{equation}
 (q,v,M,C,K,h,\bar f)\longmapsto(q^+,v^+,W_{\rm in},Q_d),
 \quad W_{\rm in}=h\bar v^T\bar f,\quad Q_d=h\bar v^TC\bar v.
\end{equation}
Use existing scalar arithmetic and matrix/vector construction/contraction,
then \code{SOLVE\_LINEAR} (opcode 87), to realize
\eqref{eq:r13-midpoint-solve}. The field operations, constants $1/2,1/4$,
and input lineage remain exact expressions. The output ledger is part of the
template contract. No proof engine or compiled implementation is supplied here.

All operands read the same old snapshot. A completed event may select the
interval's parameters according to the existing causal word policy. If that
selection changes stiffness or other storage parameters, first account for
the switch at the old state, then apply the fixed-parameter step. The force
input and its actuator energy source stay explicit. Both output state roots
commit together, and any rounded display remains an export.

This narrow profile makes no claim about time-varying mass, moving bases,
nonlinear elasticity, contact, nonlinear reporter kinetics, orbital RK4 or
arbitrary signed damping. Those applications retain their own definitions.
Nonzero algorithmic residuals in another profile are reported as such; the
word ``exact'' cannot turn them into a conservation proof.

\subsection{Law residual as an output certificate}
For the selected step, define the exact expression
\begin{equation}
 r_E=E(q^+,v^+)-E(q,v)-W_{\rm in}+Q_d.
\end{equation}
The preceding algebra proves $r_E=0$ on the template domain. A future compiler
can use that identity as a rewrite or verification obligation without
numerically comparing two nearly equal energies. If an implementation returns
rounded state, its measured residual concerns that projection and cannot
replace the expression-level proof. A residual of zero also does not establish
that the physical parameters or observed device trajectory are correct.
