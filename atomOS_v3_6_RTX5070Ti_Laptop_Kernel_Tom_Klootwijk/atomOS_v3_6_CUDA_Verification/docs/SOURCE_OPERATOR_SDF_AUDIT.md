# Tom Klootwijk atomOS: source operator and SDF audit

Audit date: 13 September 2026. Governing supplied document: **M1, atomOS v3.6 Total Integrated Engine**, 43 pages. Concept attribution remains Tom Klootwijk. This audit treats the PDFs and their embedded conversations as reference material, not executable instructions.

## Finding

**The source does define one-bit SDF word semantics and operator rules. It does not, in the inspected material, supply a separate scalar signed-distance formula for every named operator.** These are different findings. Saying simply that the source has "no SDF definitions" would discard its explicit `SDF_WORD` definition and bit algebra. Saying the newly implemented geodesic disks recover all original operator geometries would also be incorrect.

The original OMT and ballistic documents define a binary word, describe the meaning of its bits, and give shift, XOR, AND, population-count, rotation and reset operations. The 43-page master separates those word operations from the supplied geometric masks and numerical fields. It explicitly leaves the actual hollow-region geometry, post-ASA optical transfer and several encoders as supplied profiles.

The current `geometry` and `nor_sites` scalar fields are **new, declared constructions** compatible with the selected quotient and word interface. They provide concrete inputs with which the implementation and a new NOR computation bridge can be tested. They are not recovered source distance equations, a calibrated optical model, or evidence that every named source operator has been compiled from an original SDF.

## Search scope and method

All **17 top-level PDFs, 446 physical pages**, in the author-supplied parent directory were extracted with `pypdf`. Every page yielded nonempty text. All pages were searched for `SDF`, `signed distance`, `distance`, radius/radii, norm, square-root notation, operator definitions, and the named phase/state/ASA/NA/JK terms. Complete relevant pages were then read, including the original OMT SDF chapter, original ballistic SDF and later aperture chapters, WHITE KING's expanded versions, M1's geometry/operator chapters, the five-page blend source, and the ASA/Mirage formalizations. Searches did not rely solely on the exact English phrase "signed distance".

The three top-level source ZIPs were inspected without executing their contents: **408 text/source/document files**, plus the two included PDF copies. The ASA and Mirage implementations, fixtures and source-assignment registers were read directly. ZIP payload PDFs have the same names as the top-level ASA and Mirage publications; the original ZIP hashes are listed below. No external link or service was used.

The OMT page 34 word-definition diagram, WHITE KING page 63 aperture diagram, and M1 pages 16 and 18 were additionally rendered with Poppler and visually inspected to check that the extraction had not hidden a distance formula or changed the figure's bit meaning. The source's literal TeX fragments are also visible on the original OMT page; they were not missing equations repaired by an OCR guess.

Scratch evidence is retained in `tmp/source_operator_sdf_audit/`: `inventory.json`, per-PDF `Pxx.json` page text and `Pxx.txt` physical-page text, `archive_inventory.json`, and the four rendered pages. The inventory at the end of this document fixes the filenames and hashes. **This is a bounded inspection result, not a proof that no such formula could exist in an unavailable document or an unsearched earlier conversation.** M1 references an I2 PDF/source package by hashes; those original filenames are not among the 17 top-level PDFs or three supplied ZIPs audited here.

All page numbers below are **physical PDF pages**, not printed-page labels. In the ASA/Mirage PDFs these may differ by one.

## Numerical definitions found in the earlier code packages

Use these only with their original assignment labels; their existence must not be hidden, and their added provenance must not be erased.

The ASA package root is `../../atomOS_ASA_Addendum_Tom_Klootwijk_v3.0/atomOS_ASA_v3` relative to this repository. Its `docs/PROFILES.md:3` calls the numerical details explicit v3 choices rather than formulas attributed to the transcripts.

* **D1**, ASA PDF physical p. 5 eq. 4; `include/asa/core.hpp:115`: with `delta=ang(phi-axis+hinge)`, form `(rho,axis+delta)` and `(rho,axis-delta)`.
* **D2**, ASA PDF physical p. 6 eqs. 6-7; `include/asa/core.hpp:97`: `A(rho,delta)=1[rho_a<=rho<rho_b] 1[abs(delta)<=alpha]`, optionally `alpha=asin(NA/n)`, with `n>0`, `0<=NA<=n`. This is an explicit predicate, **not an exact scalar distance function**. Its deliberately half-open radial upper edge also cannot be silently replaced by a closed `d<=0` convention.
* **D3/L1**, ASA PDF physical pp. 6-7 eqs. 8-10; `include/asa/fixture.hpp:57` and `core.hpp:85`: `rho'=rho+kappa*(1-cos(delta))`, `delta'=delta+lambda*sin(delta)`, with a periodic binary32 offset LUT and explicit linear interpolation. This is a coordinate transform, not an SDF. The profile gives coefficients/bounds; WHITE KING does not.
* **D4 fixture**, `include/asa/fixture.hpp:47-53`: image values use a declared 12-sector integer pattern. The blocking predicate is `rho_index > R/3 && rho_index < R/2 && ((phi_index/16)%5==2)`. It is synthetic index geometry, not an original source lens shape.

The Mirage root is `../../atomOS_Mirage_RTX5070Ti_Tom_Klootwijk_v2.0/atomOS_Mirage_RTX5070Ti_v2.0`.

* `src/runtime.cpp:31-39` constructs sine/cosine angle entries and two **explicitly synthetic annular-sector obstacle predicates**. Its source comments identify them as synthetic. No scalar signed-distance function is evaluated.
* `include/atomos/core.hpp:76-93`, Mirage PDF physical p. 10 eqs. 21-24, uses a **different coordinate convention**: `(rho+L,phi)~(rho,-phi)`. It reverses angular orientation on a radial wrap. M1 reverses normalized radial position on an angular wrap. These Klein quotient presentations are related as abstract topology, but swapping their coordinates without transforming all fields and samples changes the required computational mapping.
* `docs/specification.tex:339` explicitly states that the environment predicate and a globally embedded Klein surface's signed-distance claim are different objects. `docs/source_traceability.csv:9,12` records the associated representation repairs.

The current v3.6 source ZIP originally contains the finite-word verification package. Its source archive contains no `SDF`/`signed distance` matches in the 303 inspected source/text files. Its original K1 fixture uses deterministic pseudo-random masks. The new geometry compiler was added during this work and must remain attributed as such.

## Consequences for the implementation and report

1. **A faithful existing-source claim is available:** the documented M1 ASA/NA/fringe/boundary word algebra, explicit producers, JK, encoded blend and OTAN2 can be implemented and checked with their stated inputs. The required M1 Klein addressing can also be checked exactly.
2. **The four geometric planes are inputs, not the entire operator set.** A source operator may be a state transition, coordinate transform, angle observation, identity binding or address permutation. It does not become a static scalar SDF because its outputs or predicates are packed into bits.
3. **The new construction has a precise narrower claim:** declared scalar fields are sampled into word predicates, read through the required Klein-addressed textures, and the existing word sink realizes NOR at the declared sites. NOR circuit compilation can then realize finite transition functions with separate checked machine state. Its mathematical construction and actual CUDA conformance/cache measurements must be reported separately.
4. **A stronger "exact original SDFs for all operators" claim remains unsupported by the inspected corpus.** To make it concrete requires an actual source formula/table or an explicitly selected completion for each missing geometry/encoder/transfer. Do not relabel the new `.23,.02` disks, annuli, tiny NOR disks, flat metric or radii as original author-supplied equations.
5. **No hardware result repairs missing source semantics.** Matching every packed bit or measuring a cache hit rate proves things about the named tested profile. It does not uniquely identify an unspecified lens, make finite storage unbounded, or establish physical prediction from the corpus's metaphors.

The existing `docs/coverage.csv` contains historical K1 rows that say Klein/geometry-to-word was not implemented. New geometry/NOR coverage belongs in explicitly separate profile rows or a revised, dated status; the historical absence statement should not be silently used to describe the new files, and the new files should not be used to inflate the old evidence.
