Subatomic particles inside a cloud chamber do not follow a classic ballistic 
trajectory (parabolic path dictated by gravity); instead, they travel in straight, 
deflected, or tightly curved paths governed by electromotive forces, particle mass, 
and collisions with air molecules. [1, 2] 
Because the subatomic particles (like alpha particles, beta particles, and muons) move 
at relativistic or near-relativistic speeds, the downward acceleration of Earth's gravity 
has a completely negligible effect on their path over the few centimeters they travel 
inside a chamber. [1, 2] 
 
Why Subatomic Paths Differ from Ballistic Trajectories 
When an invisible charged particle shoots through the supersaturated alcohol vapor of 
a cloud chamber, it knocks electrons off air molecules, creating a path of ions. The 
vapor condenses on these ions, creating a visible "cloud" track that maps its exact line 
of flight. [1, 2]
