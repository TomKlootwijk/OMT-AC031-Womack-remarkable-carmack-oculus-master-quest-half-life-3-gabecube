When a helicopter rotor appears standing still on video, it is actually an optical illusion 
called the stroboscopic effect, not the Doppler effect. [1, 2] 
The Stroboscopic Effect 
• Frame Rate Match: A camera records video by taking rapid still images, usually 
24, 30, or 60 frames per second. 
• Locked Position: When the rotor's speed in revolutions per second matches the 
camera's frame rate (or a multiple of it), each photo is snapped when the blade is 
in the exact same spot. [1, 2] 
• The Illusion: Because the blades return to the identical position for every single 
frame, your brain thinks they are completely frozen, even though they are 
spinning hundreds of times per minute. You can watch an example of this 
phenomenon in the Helicopter Rises Video. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
Why It Is Not the Doppler Effect 
• Sound vs. Sight: The Doppler effect changes the pitch of a sound when a source 
moves toward or away from a listener. 
• Motion Illusion: A stationary-looking rotor is purely a visual artifact of digital 
sampling and frame rates. 
• Real Motion: The Doppler effect does apply to the chugging sound of the blades 
as they rotate toward and away from your ear, but it has nothing to do with why 
the blades look still on a screen. [1, 2, 3]

Where the Confusion Happens: "Doppler Aliasing" 
There is one specific crossover where the Doppler effect and your FPS problem meet as 
an "artifact, " but it happens in medical ultrasound, not video cameras. [1] 
When a doctor uses a Doppler ultrasound to measure fast-flowing blood, the machine 
sends out sound pulses at a certain "frame rate" (sampling frequency). If the blood 
moves too fast, the Doppler shift exceeds the machine's sampling limit (the Nyquist 
limit). [1, 2, 3] 
The result? An aliasing artifact where the blood physically moving forward suddenly 
looks like it's flowing backward on the monitor. This is the exact same math that makes 
a helicopter blade look frozen or spin backward on a 60 FPS camera! [1, 2, 3, 4] 
So, while the helicopter illusion isn't a Doppler effect, it is driven by the exact same 
mathematical rules of sampling limits that cause Doppler tracking systems to glitch 
out. [1]
